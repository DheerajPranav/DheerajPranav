# Hey, I'm Dheeraj Pranav

AI/ML engineer, ~6 years spent moving research out of notebooks and into things people actually use. Currently doing AI Engineering at **Novartis**, where I build GenAI pipelines, RAG systems, and multi-agent workflows.

Lately I've gotten less interested in agents that just *act*, and more interested in agents you can *trust*. The kind that show their work, cite their sources, and let you inspect why they did what they did. Autonomy is easy; auditability is the hard part, and it's the part that decides whether anyone lets your system near a real decision. Most of what I'm building right now is an attempt to take that idea seriously.

I also write [**NeuroTusks**](https://neurotusks.substack.com/), twice-weekly deep dives on ML, GenAI, and agentic systems. Fittingly, it's published end to end by an agent pipeline I built, so the newsletter about autonomous agents is itself written by one.

Always happy to talk shop, argue about whether "agentic" means anything, or trade notes over chai.

## How I think about the work

A few things I keep coming back to:

- **Inspectable beats impressive.** A system nobody can reason about is worse than a simpler one they can.
- **Deterministic where it matters.** Let the model propose, but don't let it do the arithmetic you can verify.
- **Measure, don't vibe.** If I can't test it end to end and score it against reality, I don't really know if it works.
- **Draft, not send.** Agents should hand a human the decision, not take it.

Still figuring most of this out. That's the fun part.
